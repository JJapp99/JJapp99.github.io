Research question

What does it take for people to begin, and keep, the exercise they need?

M.S. Student in Exercise Physiology at Ewha Womans University

Publications Research Scholar ORCID CV

I am a master's student in Exercise Physiology at Ewha Womans University, advised by Kyuwan Lee in the Exercise Physiology Laboratory. With an undergraduate background in psychology, I combine exercise physiology with behavioral science to understand why people start—and stop—exercising.

My current work focuses on home-based isometric exercise for hypertension. I work on the ISO-BP trial, a randomized controlled trial run with the Division of Cardiology at Korea University Guro Hospital, where I study individualized intensity prescription and app- and wearable-based adherence monitoring. I built the participant-facing mobile app used in the trial, and I am the Principal Investigator of a National Research Foundation of Korea Master's Student Research Grant (2026–2027) on isometric exercise snacks.

Before graduate school, I studied psychology at Chungbuk National University, worked in sales and procurement at SERVEONE (LG Group), and then returned to school to study Health Convergence and Kinesiology at Ewha, coaching clients as a personal trainer and Pilates instructor along the way. My long-term goal is to identify the barriers to starting and maintaining exercise, and to build behavioral support strategies that help people make exercise part of daily life.
